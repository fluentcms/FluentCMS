## Changes that break code built before this preview

- A grant with a row rule gives content permissions alone. A grant of `*`, `users.manage`, `roles.manage`, a `settings.{area}.manage` or a plugin's permission with a `rowRule` held that permission in full and now holds it nowhere, and gives a plugin's `ResourcesOf` no resource; the content a `*` with a rule reaches stays under the rule. A user, a key or a member who held such a permission only through such a grant loses it, which can take `roles.manage` or `users.manage` from an administrator; Super Admin, whose `*` has no constraints, keeps everything. To find the grants, read `GET /api/management/v1/roles` as a Super Admin, which lists the roles of every site, and look for a grant with `constraints.rowRule` whose `permission` does not start with `content.`. Give the permission without the rule to keep what it gave, or `content.*.*` with the rule for what `*` with it meant (#3443).
- `POST /roles`, `PUT /roles/{id}` and the import of roles refuse a row rule with 422 `FCMS3042` on `grants[n].constraints.rowRule` beside a permission that is not of content alone (`*`, `*.*.update`, a core, settings or plugin permission), and on a content type the rule is not registered for, which for a pattern of every type, such as `content.*.update`, is any content type of the schema; such a role was saved, and the rule then matched no row of that type. A role stored with such a grant is still read and exported, but an edit or an import that keeps the grant is refused until the grant or its rule is dropped (#3443).
